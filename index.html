01 — Hardware

Ours. Down to the board.

Designed around the Rockchip RK3588 — eight cores and an onboard NPU, silicon usually reserved for flagship tablets, not phones — powered by an RK806-1 PMIC plus three dedicated 6A bucks — the two big-core clusters and the NPU are hungry enough to need their own regulators, so it's four power ICs before a single capacitor. We're not licensing a reference design and skinning it. We're drawing our own schematic and laying out our own 6-layer, 147×77mm carrier board from a blank sheet. The block diagram and target spec are done; the net-level schematic and board layout are the work in front of us.

02 — Power

A battery that tops itself up.

A thin-film solar band sits under the rear shell, feeding a dedicated MPPT controller. Our own math says it adds roughly 0.6–0.9 W in good light, so it refills the battery between charges; it cannot run the phone by itself. Heat from the chip is spread out through the frame, not turned into power: real heat-to-electricity parts only make milliwatts at a phone's temperatures, so there is no thermal generator in the first board. The battery sits in a tool-free removable tray and charges over magnetic Qi2-style wireless charging. Whether there is also a wired port is still being decided.

03 — Software

Our own OS. Running today.

Veynor OS is software we wrote from scratch in our own language, and it already runs in a web browser on a phone, tablet or computer: Messages, Notes, Maps, Files, Photos, Reminders, an assistant and more, with a lock screen, Focus modes and an emergency SOS. It is not on a Veynor Solis yet, because the phone does not exist. The first phone builds are planned to wrap Veynor OS in an Android shell; that work has not started. Veynor Pay lets two Veynor accounts send each other payment requests. No bank or card is connected, so no money moves yet, and it is not NFC tap-to-pay.

04 — Longevity

Designed to be upgraded.

The plan is a modular board: when a new revision arrives, you fit it and keep the shell. That is a design goal, not something we have proven. It is only proven once a second board revision actually fits the first shell, and we will say so here when it does.

— Why Not Just Buy a Flagship?

Sealed shut vs. built to open.

Typical flagship

✕Battery glued in — a swap means heat guns and adhesive strips, if it's possible at all.
✕RAM and storage soldered to the board — what you buy is what you're stuck with forever.
✕Charging port is a physical wear point — and a water-ingress risk over years of use.
✕Waste heat is purely a problem to dissipate, never a resource.

Veynor Solis

✓Battery sits in a tool-free removable tray — bottom third, largest footprint, no adhesive.
✓Modular board (planned) — swap in a new revision, keep the shell you already have.
✓Magnetic wireless charging — the planned way to charge without a port to wear out.
✓A solar band slowly tops up the battery between charges.
